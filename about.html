Biography

Success is not final. Failure is not fatal. It is the courage to continue that counts.
-Winston Churchill

Matthew LaMontagne was born in 1986 and raised primarily in Prospect, CT. Throughout his early years, he showed a passion for history and a keen interest in computers. In the beginning his interest in history won out. He initially dreamed of being a history teacher and went to school to become one.

Right around the time that he graduated college, the Great Recession took place. Overnight the number of teaching positions available dropped dramatically. It quickly became apparent that most people in his field were not having any success in finding work. Matthew decided that the best choice then was to work as a substitute teacher in Waterbury.

He was given a long term Technology Education substitute assignment in 2013. During that time he got to work sourcing and repairing older laptops and towers to give students equipment to work with. Budgets were tight, and he was not content with simply giving the kids paperwork.

After that year, the school opened up a series of computer teacher postions aimed at hiring dedicated certified staff. Matthew was offered an official teaching position in 2014. He wasted no time in constructing curriculum for the new course and learning the ins and outs of the Computer Information Systems standards.

Matthew's angle in teaching is to lead by example. When the district said that it needed students to have typing skill, he spent two years relearning how to type until he could perform at a professional level, and wrote a course around it. When the district noted a lack of trained computer science teachers, he spent years learning HTML/CSS/JS/SQL and eventually took and passed the Computer Science Praxis II. He learned by writing his own programs, making mistakes and sharing the process with the students.

During the Pandemic in 2020, Matthew realized right away that most families in his district were not well equipped to jump into distance learning. Most did not have computer towers or laptops at home, and the achievement gap would widen with each passing day that the children were not engaged in learning. Since Matthew was one of the few teachers who had already adopted Google Classroom well in advance of the pandemic, he knew that he had to lead the charge.

Over the course of a month, Matthew contacted hundreds of households by phone, and walked families through connecting through everything from smart phones to Playstation consoles. If it had an internet connection, he helped them make it happen. Likewise he created dozens of training tutorial videos for teachers and students to get them up to speed about Google Classroom, Google Meet, video editing software and a wide variety of digital tools to keep the work fun and engaging.

As a result of his efforts during the pandemic, Matthew was awarded with the 2020-2021 Teacher of the Year award.

People often assume that success is a straight line from major to career. However, it should be noted that we can become whatever we want at any age if we are willing to put the work in. Learning does not stop when we become adults. Instead it is the time we can best leverage our most powerful computer - our brains - to become something more.
